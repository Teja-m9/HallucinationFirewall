History of Western Music

Western classical music has evolved through distinct periods, each with unique 
characteristics, instruments, and compositional styles.

Musical Periods:

Medieval Period (500-1400):
Music was primarily religious, with Gregorian chant being the dominant form.
Music notation was developed by Guido of Arezzo around 1000 CE. Polyphony 
(multiple melodic lines) emerged in the 9th century.

Renaissance Period (1400-1600):
Music became more expressive and harmonically complex. Instruments like the 
lute and harpsichord gained popularity. Notable composers include Giovanni 
Palestrina and Josquin des Prez.

Baroque Period (1600-1750):
Characterized by ornate melodies and complex harmonies. The orchestra was 
established as a standard ensemble. Johann Sebastian Bach (1685-1750) and 
George Frideric Handel (1685-1759) were prominent composers. Antonio Vivaldi 
composed "The Four Seasons" around 1720.

Classical Period (1750-1820):
Emphasized clarity, balance, and form. The symphony and string quartet became 
popular. Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791) composed over 600 works including 
41 symphonies. Ludwig van Beethoven (1770-1827) bridged Classical and Romantic periods.

Romantic Period (1820-1900):
Featured emotional expression and nationalism. The piano became the dominant 
solo instrument. Notable composers include Frédéric Chopin, Franz Liszt, Johannes 
Brahms, and Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, who composed "The Nutcracker" in 1892.

20th Century:
Music became more experimental and diverse. Igor Stravinsky's "The Rite of Spring" 
(1913) caused a riot at its premiere due to its radical rhythms. Jazz emerged in 
New Orleans around 1900. Rock and roll developed in the 1950s.

The Beatles formed in Liverpool in 1960 and became the best-selling music act of 
all time. They released "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" in 1967.

Musical instruments are classified into families: strings (violin, cello), 
woodwinds (flute, clarinet), brass (trumpet, trombone), and percussion (drums, timpani).
