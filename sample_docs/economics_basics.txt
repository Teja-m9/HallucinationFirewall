Economics Basics

Economics is the social science that studies the production, distribution, and 
consumption of goods and services. It examines how individuals, businesses, 
governments, and nations make choices about allocating resources.

Key Economic Concepts:

Supply and Demand:
Supply refers to the quantity of goods producers are willing to sell at various 
prices. Demand refers to the quantity consumers are willing to buy at various 
prices. The equilibrium price is where supply equals demand.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP):
GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced within a country in 
a specific period. It is calculated as: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M), where C is 
consumption, I is investment, G is government spending, X is exports, and M is imports.

Inflation:
Inflation is the rate at which the general price level rises over time. It is 
measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI). The Federal Reserve targets a 2% 
annual inflation rate for the United States economy.

Unemployment:
The unemployment rate measures the percentage of the labor force without jobs 
and actively seeking work. Types include frictional, structural, and cyclical 
unemployment. Full employment is generally considered to be around 4-5% unemployment.

Economic Systems:
- Capitalism: Private ownership and free markets determine production
- Socialism: Government owns major industries, distributes goods
- Mixed Economy: Combines elements of capitalism and socialism

Central Banks:
The Federal Reserve (Fed) is the central bank of the United States, established 
in 1913. The European Central Bank (ECB) manages monetary policy for the Eurozone.
Central banks control money supply and interest rates to achieve economic stability.

Fiscal Policy involves government spending and taxation decisions. Monetary Policy 
involves central bank actions to control money supply and interest rates.

The Great Depression (1929-1939) was the worst economic downturn in modern history.
It began with the stock market crash on October 29, 1929 (Black Tuesday).
