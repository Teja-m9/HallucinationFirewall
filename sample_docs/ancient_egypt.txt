Ancient Egypt

Ancient Egypt was a civilization in Northeast Africa concentrated along the Nile 
River. Egyptian civilization began around 3100 BCE when Upper and Lower Egypt 
were unified under King Narmer (also known as Menes).

Egyptian history is divided into major periods:
- Old Kingdom (2686-2181 BCE): Age of pyramid building
- Middle Kingdom (2055-1650 BCE): Classical period of art and literature
- New Kingdom (1550-1070 BCE): Egypt's imperial age

The Pyramids of Giza:
The Great Pyramid of Giza was built for Pharaoh Khufu around 2560 BCE. It stood 
at 146.5 meters tall for over 3,800 years as the tallest man-made structure.
The pyramid contains an estimated 2.3 million stone blocks weighing 2.5 tons each.

The Sphinx:
The Great Sphinx of Giza was carved from limestone bedrock around 2500 BCE. It 
has the body of a lion and the head of a human, likely representing Pharaoh Khafre.
The Sphinx is 73 meters long and 20 meters high.

Egyptian Writing:
Hieroglyphics were used from around 3200 BCE. The Rosetta Stone, discovered in 
1799, allowed Jean-François Champollion to decipher hieroglyphics in 1822.

Notable Pharaohs:
- Khufu (Cheops): Built the Great Pyramid
- Hatshepsut: One of the few female pharaohs, ruled 1479-1458 BCE
- Tutankhamun: Became pharaoh at age 9, tomb discovered by Howard Carter in 1922
- Ramesses II: Ruled for 66 years (1279-1213 BCE), known as Ramesses the Great
- Cleopatra VII: Last active ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt, died in 30 BCE

Mummification was practiced to preserve bodies for the afterlife. The process 
took 70 days and involved removing organs, drying the body with natron salt, 
and wrapping it in linen bandages.

The Nile River floods annually from July to November, depositing fertile silt 
that enabled agriculture. Ancient Egyptians developed a 365-day calendar based 
on the Nile's flooding cycle.
